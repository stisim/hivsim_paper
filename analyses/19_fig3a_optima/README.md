# 19: Does our Optima reproduce the Optima HIV panel of Figure 3A?

**Question:** Bansi-Matharu et al. 2025 (Lancet Glob Health 13:e1006–19), Figure 3A, Malawi, Optima HIV: the share of new infections by the source partner's treatment status (undiagnosed; diagnosed but treatment naive; receiving treatment; not receiving treatment after having started), and 8,459 new infections in 2024. Does our rerun of the Optima HIV Malawi project (`models/optima/reference/Malawi_20220912.prj`, Optima HIV 2.12.2) give the same shares and totals, and does Optima′ (`models/optima/v4/`) give the same as our Optima? Robyn's focus year is 2020 (`docs/core_question.md`); Optima′ is verified only for 2000–2020 (`docs/optima_prime_plan.md`).

**Published values:** `common/fig3a_malawi.csv` (digitised, ±1–2 points, by analysis 18). Optima HIV, 2020: undiagnosed 45.2%, diagnosed never treated 28.2%, on ART 17.6%, interrupted 9.0%; 2024: 50.5 / 5.3 / 35.6 / 8.5%.

**How the source's state is counted in Optima.** Optima's force of infection is a sum over source compartments (cascade state × CD4 × population): `model.py` already records new infections by the source's state and population (`raw_incibypop`, sexual infections from the force of infection plus mother-to-child transmission spread over the mother's states). Optima's states are undiagnosed, diagnosed (not in care), in care (pre-ART), on ART unsuppressed, on ART suppressed, and lost to follow-up. Mapped to the paper's categories in two ways:
- **States** (what Optima can report directly): undiagnosed → undiagnosed; diagnosed + in care → diagnosed never treated; on ART (suppressed or not) → on ART; lost → interrupted. Optima's `lost` holds both people lost before ART (from care) and after (from ART), and its in-care state also takes in people who stopped ART (when the number on ART falls, and returners from lost).
- **History** (the paper's definition, `common/transmission.py`, swap design D8): care and lost split by whether the person has ever been on ART. Optima doesn't carry that, so `optima_attr.py` adds a tracer: the number of ever-treated people in each care and lost compartment, moved by exactly the same operations as Optima's people (the step's transition matrix, ageing and risk moves, population forcing, the cascade rules moving people on and off ART and between care and lost, taken proportionally from each compartment, as Optima does). Ever-treated people in care or lost → interrupted; the rest → diagnosed never treated. The tracer changes no dynamics: it is checked that the patched model gives identical results.
- Counted: **sexual** transmissions (primary; the paper's network models count only sexual) and **all** including mother-to-child, reported separately. Optima's annual numbers are its rate in the step starting at each whole year (its own `numinci` convention); the mean over the year's 5 steps is reported too.

**Optima′:** the same attribution by source state added as an output of its force of infection (`Transmission`), and compared with Optima by state (Optima′'s `lost` is not split: the history mapping is checked for Optima only, unless the ever-treated split proves to matter, in which case the tracer's share is applied). Tolerance: Optima′'s ~2% on new infections, 2000–2020.

**What's run:**
- `run_optima.py` (env `optima`): the patched model, saving `outputs/optima_by_source.csv` (year, mapping, counted, category, number, share) for 1990–2030 (2021–2030 is projection with Optima's inputs held as in the project: flagged).
- `run_prime.py` (env `hp_rc3`): Optima′ to 2025 at dt = 0.02, `outputs/prime_by_source.csv`.
- `compare.py`: table and figure against the published shares and totals (`figures/fig3a_optima.png`).

**Expected:** the published Optima run was MIHPSA's, with treatment inputs beyond 2020 that our project doesn't have, and our project's new infections in 2024 are 29,191 (`optima_malawi_totals.csv`), 3.5× the published 8,459, so the published run is probably a different (later) calibration; shares may still be close in 2020. The published Optima shows 3.7% "interrupted" in 2000, when almost nobody had started ART, which suggests the published mapping is the states one (lost → interrupted).
